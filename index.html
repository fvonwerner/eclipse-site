Screen time you keep, together.

Eclipse puts a daily limit on the apps you choose. Stay inside it and grow a streak. Put the phone down on purpose and a few friends are doing it with you.

The wall

When time is up, it is up.

Once today's budget is spent, your tracked apps close behind a quiet screen. There are two ways forward from it, and neither one is a “keep scrolling anyway” button.

  • Close it

    Put the phone down and the day counts as kept. Your streak grows by one. Reaching your limit is a success, so the whole app stays green when you land on it.

  • Take two minutes

    For the message that genuinely cannot wait: two minutes, granted the moment you tap, in the app you tapped from. Three a day, then a cooldown. Your streak survives all three.

And if you need a real exception, one friend can open it for you. You name a single anchor, and only their gift of time unlocks you. Days an anchor helped are their own calm blue on your calendar: never a kept day you did not earn, never a broken streak either.

Phone down

The good version of a competition.

Lock the whole phone, on purpose

Start a session and everything closes, not just the apps you track. Invite whoever is already down and you finish together.

A weekly board that counts the right thing

It ranks the minutes you chose to be unreachable, never the hours you spent scrolling. Nobody wins by having a worse week.

Finish and get paid in fuel

A finished session pays fuel, and friends still down at the end pay you a bonus. Fuel buys 3D bodies to keep on your home screen: asteroids up to galaxies, 33 of them. It buys nothing else. Money and fuel can never buy time through the wall.

Stopping early pays nothing

The session tells you so before you confirm. The minutes still count on the board, because you were still off your phone for them.

Your circle

Small, invited, and honest.

Every person claims a short tag. You add a friend by typing theirs, they approve it, and that is the entire join flow. No feed, no follower count, no strangers.

What a friend sees is the promise you made and whether you kept it: your daily limit, your streak, how recent days ended, and the minutes you chose to put the phone down. What they never see is your screen time, the apps you picked, or anyone else in your circle.

When you really are stuck, you can ask the circle for time on the wall of shame, out loud, to the people you picked. The first yes ends it. It is meant to be slightly embarrassing, which is most of why it works.

Private by construction.

Your screen time is measured by iOS itself, on your phone, and it does not leave. Eclipse has no server and no analytics SDK. Even inside the app, Apple's frameworks hand it coarse threshold events rather than a record of what you looked at.

The circle is optional, and it costs nothing to skip: a fresh install creates no account and no cloud data at all until you claim a tag or add someone. When you do, each friendship gets its own private Apple CloudKit share, so a friend can only ever read the one made for the two of you.

And the day turns over at six in the morning, not at midnight, because a budget that refills at 00:00 refills exactly when you least need it to.
